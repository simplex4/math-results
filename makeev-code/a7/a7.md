**I did not find a fatal mathematical error in the proposed all-dimensional proof.** I did find a false normalization sentence, several arguments whose presentation needed clarification, and some smaller editorial issues. I revised the manuscript, expanded the exact-algebra tests, and compiled the revised paper.

My assessment is that **the main argument survives this review**. The revisions retain the theorem for every \(d\ge 4\) and its degree bound \(3d(d+1)-3\); they do not replace a failed proof with a weaker result. This is not proof-assistant certification or independent human verification, and the computational checks alone do not establish the theorem.

## What I corrected

### 1. The statement that all evaluations are at unit vectors is false

The manuscript says that “all function evaluations in this paper are at unit vectors.” That is true of the root-label evaluations, but not of the auxiliary evaluations at the simplex vertices. 

Indeed, with

$$
p_i=A\left(e_i-\frac1N\mathbf1\right),
$$

we have

$$
\|p_i\|^2=\frac{d}{d+1},
$$

whereas

$$
\frac{p_i-p_j}{\sqrt2}=A\alpha_{ij}
$$

is a unit vector.

I changed the statement to **“all root-label evaluations”** and explicitly distinguished them from the vertex evaluations. This is a wording error, not an error in the polynomial calculations: the factors \(1/(2\sqrt2)\) and \(1/(4\sqrt2)\) used in the cubic and quintic formulas check out.

### 2. The universal-cover body and its normalization needed to be explicit

The original definition of \(C_d\) is correct, but “appropriately scaled polar” leaves the scale unstated.  I added the identity

$$
\boxed{C_d=\frac1{\sqrt2}(\Delta-\Delta)^\circ,}
$$

where the polar is taken in \(V=\mathbf1^\perp\) and

$$
\Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{e_i-N^{-1}\mathbf1\}.
$$

I also clarified the allowed congruences and distinguished the **polar of the difference body** from the difference body itself. This matches the regular-simplex universal-cover conjecture stated by Kuperberg. ([NYJM][1])

### 3. The spectral-fiber argument was correct but too compressed

The important issue in Lemma 4.4 is not just that rank-one perturbations have a secular equation. One must explain why a repeated eigenvalue cannot leave a continuous family of possible vectors \(c\).

The original argument contains the necessary ingredients: separated eigenvalue clusters, persistence of the double eigenvalues, and the residue calculation.  I expanded the explanation to make explicit that:

* every repeated eigenvalue of \(B\) is an unchanged double eigenvalue of \(S\);
* cancellation of the corresponding pole forces the projection of \(c\) onto that eigenspace to vanish;
* on each remaining, one-dimensional eigenspace, \(c\) is determined up to sign.

The revision explicitly includes zero components and \(c=0\). These are clarifications of the existing proof, not additional assumptions.

### 4. A coordinate change could be mistaken for an unauthorized symmetry

In Case 2 of the normal-form proof, the substitution

$$
r=\frac{x-y}{\sqrt2},\qquad s=\frac{x+y}{\sqrt2}
$$

is a **passive change of coordinates**. It is not, by itself, an assertion that the original formulas for both \(Q\) and \(H\) are preserved. The manuscript correctly rewrites the cubic in these coordinates, but the distinction deserved emphasis. 

I added the transformation rule

$$
P'=PT,\qquad G'=GT,\qquad S'=T^TST,
$$

which explains why the symmetric coefficient-matrix condition survives the coordinate change.

The actual final normalization of the placement is the allowed circle rotation \(h_{\pi/4}\), which also acts on the second weight plane. That step is correct. 

### 5. Other clarifications and editorial corrections

I displayed the symmetric coefficient matrices underlying both normal-form cases, so the signs and factors of two are directly checkable. I also made explicit that the signed orbit “strata” need not be smooth or connected, justified why the outside index set in the normal form is nonempty, clarified squared-difference notation, corrected “corollarys,” and corrected section-reference numbering.

These changes improve precision without changing the mathematical strategy.

## The crucial proof steps I checked

### The dimension count really does obtain a strict inequality

This is one of the most consequential parts of the paper. At the threshold number of roots, ordinary parameter counting would not suffice.

For a signed orbit-type piece \(Z\), let \(m\) be the number of signed orbit classes, \(\ell=\dim L_Z\), and \(p=\dim\mathcal P_d\). The spectral argument gives

$$
\dim Z+\ell\le m.
$$

The interpolation argument then gives

$$
\dim\mathcal I_Z
=\dim Z+\ell+p-m
\le p.
$$

These are the bounds used in the manuscript. 

The essential further step is that every nonempty fiber over a polynomial contains a **one-dimensional circle of placements**. The action is free on the full placement \(A\), even when some individual roots have stabilizers. Consequently, the projected bad set has dimension at most \(p-1\), yielding the needed dense complement. I found no gap in this reduction. 

I also checked that the restriction \(\|c\|<1\) belongs only to the auxiliary spectral argument; it does not inadvertently restrict the linear functional in the original agreement problem. The manuscript already states this correctly. 

### The exceptional normal-form argument appears complete

I checked both cases—an exceptional root in \(E_1\) and one in \(E_2\)—including the zero-parameter branches, the zero-mean contradictions, and the divisions that require nonzero quantities.

The necessary normal form has disjoint, balanced supports and satisfies

$$
u=\frac{x^2-y^2}{\gamma},
\qquad
\gamma^2=\frac1{k_1}+\frac1{k_2}.
$$

The manuscript only claims the information needed for exceptional cubic solutions, not a classification of all cubic solutions. That distinction is important and is preserved in the revision. 

The resulting obstruction is

$$
\boxed{\Lambda_A(H)=\frac1{\sqrt2\,k_1k_2\gamma}\ne0.}
$$

I checked the edge-group calculations and the outside-moment identity used to derive this formula. I found no missing normalization factor or sign error. The argument needs nonvanishing, not positivity, so either sign of \(\gamma\) is allowed. 

### The compactness and final perturbation steps work

The exceptional obstruction rules out a sequence of exceptional agreements for nonzero \(\varepsilon\to0\). Compactness then gives exceptional avoidance for all sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\), and a positive residual gap makes that property stable under small polynomial perturbations. 

Intersecting this open neighborhood with the dense set supplied by the nonexceptional argument produces the required \(R\). I found no improper interchange of quantifiers or unproved closedness assumption in this final combination. 

## What the computations establish—and what they do not

The original script passed **1,322 exact assertions**. The expanded script passed **4,748 exact assertions**. Both execution logs are included in the package.

The expanded tests cover more than the original normal-form examples: they include zero-\(\omega\) frames, nonconstant outside coordinates, both signs of \(\gamma\), general simplex frames, the passive coordinate transformation, quadratic reconstruction identities, and selected spectral examples. The checks use exact symbolic arithmetic, not floating-point tolerances or random sampling.

Nevertheless, **these are regression checks, not a computational proof of the classification or dimension bounds**. The original script correctly warns about that limitation, and the revised script retains it.  My assessment of the universal statements rests on checking their mathematical arguments, rather than extrapolating from those examples.

The revised PDF has 16 pages and compiles without warnings.

## What remains nonexplicit

The paper still does **not** provide coefficients for \(R\), a numerical value of \(\varepsilon_0\), or a proof that the unmodified quintic seed \(Q+\varepsilon H\) works in every dimension. It explicitly acknowledges those limitations. They are not defects in an existence theorem, but they should not be obscured when describing the result. 

**Bottom line:** I found corrections worth making, but not a reason to withdraw or weaken the all-dimensional existence claim. The two most demanding arguments—the spectral-fiber dimension bound and the exceptional normal-form proof—survived this review. The revised manuscript makes those arguments more explicit while retaining the appropriate verification caveat.
