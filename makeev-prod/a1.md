## Verdict

After a detailed audit, **I did not find a substantive mathematical error in either part**. In particular, the dimension-(4) counterexample in part 2 appears to be valid.

This is stronger than merely saying that the argument “looks plausible”: I independently reconstructed the delicate algebra in Lemma 4, including the multiplication-table identities, eliminations, and the degree-five functional. All of those checks agreed exactly with the text. This is not a formal proof-assistant certification, but I would not reject the proof on mathematical grounds.

There are, however, several **bibliographic, notational, and expository corrections** that should be made before circulating it.

## 1. Part 1

### Exact-edge-label reduction

Lemma 1 and Corollary 2 are correct. The exact edge labels form the image of
[
\delta:V\longrightarrow\mathcal E,\qquad
(\delta c)_{ij}=\frac{c_i-c_j}{\sqrt2},
]
and their orthogonal complement is
[
\mathcal C={X:X^T=-X,\ X\mathbf1=0}.
]
The triangle criterion, the orthogonality computation, and the dimensions are all consistent. 

A minor omission is that the stated dimension of (\mathcal C) uses the fact that
[
X\longmapsto X\mathbf1
]
maps the space of skew matrices onto (\mathbf1^\perp), so it has rank (m-1). This is easy to supply and is not a gap of substance.

The normalization
[
u_{ij}=\frac{e_i-e_j}{\sqrt2}
]
is necessary and correct. It is also harmless relative to formulations using the unnormalized roots, since the vector (b) can be rescaled. 

### Cubic theorem

The central identity
[
\langle Q,X\cdot p\rangle
=2\sqrt2\sum_{i<j}X_{ij}Q(u_{ij})
]
has the correct sign and factor. In particular:

[
X\cdot p=-3\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}x_i^2x_j
]
has tensor pairing
[
-3\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}(T_Q)_{iij},
]
and expansion of (Q(e_i-e_j)) gives exactly the same expression. 

The extremization argument is also correct. If
[
\Psi(A)=\langle P,A\cdot p\rangle
]
has a maximum at (A_0), then all derivatives along (A_0e^{tX}) vanish. With the stated action convention,
[
A_0^{-1}\cdot P=P\circ A_0,
]
so the resulting edge labels (P(A_0u_{ij})) are orthogonal to every (X\in\mathcal C), hence exact. The conversion from (c) to (b=A_0c) is correct. 

Thus Theorem 3 really does prove the assertion for every spherical function having only harmonic degrees (1) and (3).

### Stability theorem

The derivative
[
D\mathcal T_p(I)[X]=-\frac{3}{\sqrt2}X
]
is correct. For (u_{ij}=(e_i-e_j)/\sqrt2),
[
(Xu_{ij})*i=(Xu*{ij})*j=-\frac{X*{ij}}{\sqrt2},
]
which gives precisely the displayed derivative. Since (X\mathbf1=0), the resulting edge label is already in (\mathcal C), so the projection does nothing. 

The Brouwer-degree perturbation argument is valid. One small improvement would be to state explicitly that the inverse function theorem allows (B) to be chosen so that (0) is the unique zero of (F_p) in (\overline B). Then the identification of the local degree with the sign of the determinant is immediate. The intended argument already implicitly uses this. 

### Zonal theorem

The two-level construction is correct. The vector (c) has sum zero and norm one, and its differences take only the values (0,\pm(\alpha-\beta)). Consequently the labels (h(a\cdot Au_{ij})) are exact, with the displayed choice of (z). 

So the mathematical results in part 1 stand.

## 2. Part 2: the counterexample

The proposed function is
[
f_\varepsilon(z_1,z_2)
=\operatorname{Re}(z_1^2\overline{z_2})
+\varepsilon |z_1|^2\operatorname{Im}(z_1^2\overline{z_2}).
]
It is smooth and odd, and the claimed conclusion is that all sufficiently small nonzero (\varepsilon) are counterexamples. 

### Simplex model and exactness criterion

The cyclic vectors
[
q_j=\sqrt{\frac25},(\zeta^j,\zeta^{2j})
]
do form a regular (4)-simplex with Gram matrix
[
\langle q_i,q_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}-\frac15.
]
Thus ((q_i-q_j)/\sqrt2) is a normalized (A_4) root system.

The criterion
[
\Pi Y\Pi=0
]
for exactness is also correct. For a skew matrix (Y), this condition is equivalent to
[
Y_{ij}=\rho_i-\rho_j.
]
The frame formula
[
c=\sqrt2\sum_i\rho_iq_i
]
then gives (c\cdot u_{ij}=\rho_i-\rho_j). 

To connect this explicitly with the original statement, one should add the sentence
[
c=g^{-1}b.
]
Then (b\cdot gu_{ij}=c\cdot u_{ij}).

### Global cubic annihilation

Lemma 3 is correct. With
[
K=g^{-1}Jg,\qquad C=Q\circ g,
]
one has
[
K\cdot(Q\circ g)=(J\cdot Q)\circ g=0
]
because (Q) is invariant under
[
h_\theta(z_1,z_2)=(e^{i\theta}z_1,e^{2i\theta}z_2).
]
Skew-adjointness of the infinitesimal orthogonal action then makes
[
\langle Q\circ g,K\cdot P_3\rangle=0.
]
The cubic identity consequently gives
[
\Lambda_g(Y_Q(g))=0
]
for every (g), and the same holds for (R=\operatorname{Im}W). 

The factor (2\sqrt2) in equation (15) is correct.

## 3. The delicate part: Lemma 4

This is where an error would most likely occur. I found none.

### Frame equations

The assertions
[
\sum_i a_i^2=\sum_i b_i^2
=\sum_i a_ib_i
=\sum_i a_i\overline{b_i}=0
]
and
[
\sum_i|a_i|^2=\sum_i|b_i|^2=2
]
follow from the fact that the four real coordinate columns of the simplex frame are orthonormal.

It follows that
[
\mathbf1,a,b,\bar a,\bar b
]
are an orthogonal basis of (\mathbb C^5). The expansion formula (22) is correct provided the Hermitian convention is stated as
[
\langle u,v\rangle=\sum_i\overline{u_i}v_i,
]
conjugate-linear in the first entry and linear in the second. Without declaring this convention, equation (22) may appear conjugated to some readers. 

### Cubic exactness conditions

Expanding
[
(a_i-a_j)^2(\bar b_i-\bar b_j)
]
modulo exact matrices gives exactly
[
-a^2\wedge\bar b+2a\wedge(a\bar b).
]
After inserting the basis expansions, comparison with the complex conjugate gives
[
s=0,\qquad m\in\mathbb R,\qquad 4u=\bar n.
]
Those conditions are correct. 

### Multiplication table and equations (E1)–(E8)

I independently reconstructed the coordinatewise algebra generated by
[
a,b,\bar a,\bar b
]
using table (34). Comparing the stated associativity identities gives exactly:

[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{identity}&\text{relevant coefficients}\ \hline
(a^2)b=a(ab)&E1/8,\ -E2/8,\ -E3/8\
(ab)b=a(b^2)&E4/2,\ -E5/8\
(a^2)\bar a=a(a\bar a)&E6/20\
(ab)\bar a=a(b\bar a)&E7/16\
(ab)\bar b=a(b\bar b)&E8/20 .
\end{array}
]

Thus all eight displayed equations are correct. 

An important logical point is that these equations are only asserted to be **necessary**, not sufficient. That is enough: every actual cubic zero satisfies them, and the proof shows the transverse functional is nonzero for every candidate permitted by these necessary equations. Possible extraneous algebraic candidates would not invalidate the argument.

### Case classification

The three cases are exhaustive:

1. (t=0);
2. (t>0,\ n=0);
3. (t>0,\ n\ne0).

The deductions in Cases I and II are correct.

In Case III, the two equations in (q=m/t) and (h=|n|^2/t^2) have resultants

[
16(q-4)(q+2)(21q^2+42q+16)
\quad(\sigma=1)
]
and
[
-16(q+4)(q-2)(21q^2-42q+16)
\quad(\sigma=-1).
]

These match equations (45)–(46). The exclusions and the surviving values

[
q=-\sigma\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{105}}{21}\right),
\qquad
h=\frac{-74+14\sqrt{105}}3
]
are correct, as are the resulting values of (t^2) and (m^2). 

### Degree-five functional

I also independently expanded
[
(a_i-a_j)^3(\bar a_i-\bar a_j)(\bar b_i-\bar b_j).
]
The seven wedge terms in equation (54), together with the omitted exact term
[

reproduce the edge expression exactly.

Using table (34) and the wedge-pairing identity gives precisely

with the (\mathcal B_{\rm raw}) shown in equation (57). Its simplification to equation (59) is also correct.

The three case evaluations are:

respectively. All are nonzero. Thus
[

at every exact zero of (Q). 

As a concrete spot check, at (g=I) one is in Case I:
[

### Perturbation argument

The final sequential compactness argument is logically sound. The negation of the conclusion produces
[
\varepsilon_\nu\to0,\qquad \varepsilon_\nu\ne0,
]
and exact zeros (g_\nu). A subsequence converges to a cubic zero (g). Exactness gives
[
0=\Lambda_{g_\nu}(Y_Q(g_\nu))
+\varepsilon_\nu\Lambda_{g_\nu}(Y_H(g_\nu)).
]
The first term vanishes identically by Lemma 3, so
[
\Lambda_{g_\nu}(Y_H(g_\nu))=0.
]
Passing to the limit contradicts Lemma 4. The quantifiers are correct, including the assertion for both signs of (\varepsilon). 

The harmonic refinement is also correct: the displayed Laplacian computations make (H_5) harmonic, and subtracting ((3/5)R) does not change (\Lambda_g) because (\Lambda_g(R)=0).

## 4. Corrections that should be made

### The bibliography in part 2 is wrong

Part 2 says that “a paper posted on July 11, 2026” still states the regular-(A_d) assertion as a conjecture, but its reference `[1]` points to `arXiv:math/9809165`.  

That arXiv item is Kuperberg’s paper, submitted on September 28, 1998 and revised in 1999. ([arXiv][1])

The likely intended citation is Yizhen Chen’s **“Note on Lebesgue’s universal cover problem,” arXiv:2607.27227**, dated July 11, 2026. Its introduction explicitly says that Makeev conjectured the (A_d) polytope to be a universal cover. ([arXiv][2])

So the underlying status claim is supported, but the reference must be replaced.

### Other presentation changes

The following should be made explicit:

* In equation (22), declare the Hermitian inner-product convention.
* In Lemma 2, say (c=g^{-1}b) when translating back to the original formulation.
* In part 1’s degree proof, explicitly invoke the inverse function theorem before computing the local degree.
* Clean up the corrupted TeX, particularly the stray `*`, commas used for multiplication, and lines of repeated equals signs.
* State that the (SO(4)) and (O(4)) formulations are equivalent for the (A_4) root system because it has an orientation-reversing symmetry. This matters when relating the result to the universal-cover formulation.

I have not independently established novelty or priority; the counterexample should still be checked by specialists and circulated as a conventional mathematical manuscript. But **as a self-contained proof, I find the counterexample sound**.
